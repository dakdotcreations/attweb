### Content

Every year, thousands of patients from Uganda and across East Africa travel abroad for specialist medical care. If you're considering it for yourself or a family member, this guide explains how the process works and what to prepare.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Always discuss treatment decisions with qualified doctors.*

## Why do Ugandans travel for treatment?

- **Specialist expertise** that isn't always available locally, such as complex cardiac surgery, transplants or certain cancer treatments
- **Accredited hospitals** with national and international accreditation (for example JCI)
- **Cost:** some destinations offer treatment at significantly lower prices than private care elsewhere
- **Shorter waiting times** for planned procedures
- **Second opinions** before a major decision

## Step 1: Choose the destination around the treatment

There is no single "best" country. Compare destinations on:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Which hospitals have specialists in my condition?** | Volume and experience matter more than the country's name. |
| **What is the total cost?** | Include flights, accommodation and the length of stay, not only the hospital fee. |
| **How long is the flight?** | Long journeys can be hard for some patients. |
| **What visa is needed, and how fast is it?** | Some countries have dedicated medical visas with attendant visas. |
| **Is language a barrier?** | Check whether the hospital provides English-speaking staff or interpreters. |

Commonly chosen destinations from Uganda include **India** (wide range of specialties, English widely spoken, direct flights to Mumbai), **Turkey** (orthopaedics, cosmetic, dental and eye care), the **UAE** (short flights and modern diagnostics), and **Kenya** or **Egypt** for some procedures closer to home.

## Step 2: Get a medical opinion before you travel

You don't need to fly abroad just to find out what treatment you need. Start by gathering:

- Recent **medical reports** and your doctor's referral letter
- **Scans and imaging**, such as X-ray, CT and MRI (reports plus images where possible)
- **Lab results**
- A list of **current medications**

Through a medical facilitator such as Altura, these are shared confidentially with hospital specialists, who return a **treatment opinion, an estimated length of stay and a cost estimate**. This usually takes a few working days. Ask for opinions from more than one hospital if you want to compare.

Then ask questions:

- What exactly does the estimate include and exclude?
- How long will I be in hospital, and how long must I stay afterwards?
- What follow-up will I need at home?

Make your decision with your family and, ideally, your local doctor.

## Step 3: Apply for the right visa

Visa rules depend on the destination. Some general points:

- Many countries need an **invitation or appointment letter from the hospital**.
- Some, such as India, have a dedicated **medical visa** with linked **attendant visas** for family members.
- Your passport should usually be valid for at least six months.
- Apply early. Processing can take from a few days to several weeks.

Requirements change, so check the latest rules or ask us before applying.

## Step 4: Plan travel and accommodation

- **Flights:** if the patient has mobility problems, request **wheelchair assistance** when booking. For some conditions, the doctor must confirm you are fit to fly.
- **Accommodation:** attendants need somewhere close to the hospital, and patients often stay nearby after discharge for follow-up appointments.
- **Money:** carry a card and some cash in US dollars. Ask the hospital about payment methods and deposits in advance.

## Step 5: Treatment and recovery

On arrival, a coordinator helps with admission and paperwork. Hospitals with international patient departments usually provide interpreters, and a dedicated coordinator from your facilitator helps your family follow every step.

Allow **extra days after discharge** in your plan. Doctors normally want to see you at least once before clearing you to fly home.

## Step 6: Aftercare at home

Before leaving, make sure you have:

- A **discharge summary** and all reports
- A **medication plan** with enough supply for the journey home
- **Follow-up instructions** and a way to contact your specialist, for example by teleconsultation

Share these with your doctor in Uganda.

## How much does it cost?

Costs vary widely by treatment, country, hospital and length of stay. Budget for:

1. **Hospital and treatment fees** (the largest part)
2. **Flights** for the patient and attendants
3. **Visas**
4. **Accommodation and meals** for attendants
5. **Local transport**
6. **A contingency** of 10–20% for extra tests or a longer stay

Always ask for a **written estimate** before travelling.
