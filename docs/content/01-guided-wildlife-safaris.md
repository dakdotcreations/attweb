### Content

Uganda is often called the Pearl of Africa, and for safari travellers the name fits. In one country you can watch lions on open savannah, cruise past hundreds of hippos, track rhino on foot and trek mountain gorillas, often in the same week.

Uganda also gets far fewer visitors than Kenya or Tanzania, so sightings feel personal. You won't find twenty vehicles around one lion here.

This guide covers the parks we recommend most often, what to expect in each, and how to plan your time.

## Murchison Falls National Park

**Best for:** first-time safari travellers, big game and dramatic scenery

Uganda's largest national park is split by the Victoria Nile. North of the river, open grassland supports giraffe, elephant, buffalo, Uganda kob, hartebeest and lion. The highlight is the falls themselves: the entire Nile squeezes through a gorge just seven metres wide, and you can see it from a boat below and from a hiking trail above.

**Don't miss:** the launch cruise to the foot of the falls, and the Delta cruise for the rare shoebill stork.

## Queen Elizabeth National Park

**Best for:** boat safaris, variety and the tree-climbing lions

Queen Elizabeth sits between Lake Edward and Lake George, linked by the Kazinga Channel. A two-hour boat cruise on the channel puts you metres from hippos, buffalo, elephants and countless water birds. In the southern **Ishasha sector**, lions famously rest in the branches of fig trees. It's one of only two places in Africa where this happens regularly.

**Don't miss:** the Kazinga Channel cruise, and chimpanzee tracking in the Kyambura Gorge.

## Lake Mburo National Park

**Best for:** a short safari from Kampala, walking safaris

Only about four hours from Kampala, Lake Mburo is Uganda's best place for zebra, impala and eland. Because there are no elephants or lions in most areas, you can explore **on foot, by bicycle or on horseback**, which is a rare experience in East Africa.

## Kidepo Valley National Park

**Best for:** remote wilderness and repeat visitors

Tucked against the borders of South Sudan and Kenya, Kidepo is wild, beautiful and very quiet. Expect cheetah (the only place in Uganda to see them), large buffalo herds, ostrich and the Karamojong culture. Most guests fly in because the drive is long.

## Ziwa Rhino Sanctuary

Rhinos were wiped out in Uganda by the early 1980s. Ziwa, on the road to Murchison, is where they are being brought back. Tracking southern white rhino **on foot** with a ranger is unforgettable, and it's easy to add to a Murchison safari.

## When is the best time for a Uganda safari?

Uganda sits on the equator, so temperatures are pleasant all year (around 20–28°C in most parks). Rainfall is what changes.

- **June to September:** dry season, with the best game viewing as animals gather near water. This is also the busiest time, so book lodges and gorilla permits early.
- **December to February:** the second dry season, which is warm and excellent for both safari and trekking.
- **March to May and October to November:** the rainy seasons. Showers usually come in the afternoon, the landscapes are green, there are fewer visitors, and some lodges offer lower rates.

There's no bad month to visit. The wet months just mean muddier roads and a raincoat in your bag.

## How many days do you need?

- **3 days:** Lake Mburo, or a quick Murchison Falls trip
- **5–6 days:** Murchison Falls and Queen Elizabeth, the classic combination
- **8–10 days:** add Kibale Forest for chimpanzees and Bwindi for gorillas
- **12+ days:** the full circuit, including Kidepo or a beach extension in Zanzibar

## Tips from our guides

1. **Bring neutral colours.** Khaki, olive and beige work best, and dark blue attracts tsetse flies in some parks.
2. **Pack binoculars.** One pair per person makes a big difference.
3. **Start early.** Most predator activity happens in the first two hours after sunrise.
4. **Get the yellow fever certificate.** It's required to enter Uganda.
5. **Let your guide lead.** They talk to other guides and rangers and usually know where the animals are.
