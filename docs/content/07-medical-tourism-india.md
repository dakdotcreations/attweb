### Content

Every year, thousands of patients from Uganda and across East Africa travel to India for specialist medical care. If you're considering it for yourself or a family member, this guide explains how the process works and what to prepare.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Always discuss treatment decisions with qualified doctors.*

## Why do Ugandans choose India for treatment?

- **Specialist expertise:** India's leading hospitals have specialists in cardiac surgery, oncology, neurosurgery, orthopaedics, transplants and fertility, with high patient volumes.
- **Accredited hospitals:** many major hospitals hold national (NABH) and international (JCI) accreditation.
- **Cost:** treatment often costs significantly less than in Europe, the Middle East or private care elsewhere.
- **Shorter waiting times** for planned procedures.
- **English** is widely spoken by doctors and hospital staff.
- **Direct flight connections** from Entebbe, including Uganda Airlines to Mumbai.

## Step 1: Get a medical opinion before you travel

You don't need to fly to India just to find out what treatment you need. Start by gathering:

- Recent **medical reports** and your doctor's referral letter
- **Scans and imaging**, such as X-ray, CT and MRI (reports plus images where possible)
- **Lab results**
- A list of **current medications**

Through a medical facilitator such as Altura, these are shared confidentially with hospital specialists, who return a **treatment opinion, an estimated length of stay and a cost estimate**. This usually takes a few working days.

## Step 2: Choose your hospital and plan

Compare the opinions and estimates, then ask questions:

- What exactly does the estimate include and exclude?
- How long will I be in hospital, and how long must I stay in India afterwards?
- What follow-up will I need at home?

Make your decision with your family and, ideally, your local doctor.

## Step 3: Apply for the India e-Medical visa

India offers an **e-Medical visa** for patients, applied for online. Key points:

- You'll need an **invitation or appointment letter from the Indian hospital**.
- Up to **two attendants** (usually family members) can apply for **e-Medical Attendant visas** linked to the patient's visa.
- Your passport must be valid for at least six months.
- Apply at least **one to two weeks** before travel to allow for processing.

Requirements can change, so check the latest rules or ask us before applying.

## Step 4: Plan travel and accommodation

- **Flights:** if the patient has mobility problems, request **wheelchair assistance** when booking. For some conditions, the doctor must confirm you are fit to fly.
- **Accommodation:** attendants need somewhere close to the hospital, and patients often stay nearby after discharge for follow-up appointments. Serviced apartments and guest houses near major hospitals are practical and affordable.
- **Money:** carry a mix of a card and cash in US dollars. Ask the hospital about payment methods and deposits in advance.

## Step 5: Treatment and recovery in India

On arrival, a coordinator helps with admission and paperwork. Hospitals with international patient departments usually provide interpreters, though a dedicated coordinator from your facilitator can help your family follow every step.

Allow **extra days after discharge** in your plan. Doctors normally want to see you at least once before clearing you to fly home.

## Step 6: Aftercare at home

Before leaving India, make sure you have:

- A **discharge summary** and all reports
- A **medication plan** with enough supply for the journey home
- **Follow-up instructions** and a way to contact your specialist, for example by teleconsultation

Share these with your doctor in Uganda.

## How much does it cost?

Costs vary widely by treatment, hospital and length of stay. Budget for:

1. **Hospital and treatment fees** (the largest part)
2. **Flights** for the patient and attendants
3. **Visas**
4. **Accommodation and meals** for attendants
5. **Local transport**
6. **A contingency** of 10–20% for extra tests or a longer stay

Always ask for a **written estimate** before travelling.
