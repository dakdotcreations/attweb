### Content

For many Ugandan businesses, growth means travel: to source products directly from manufacturers, attend trade fairs, meet partners or present at conferences. Done well, a single trip can transform your supply chain. Done badly, it can be expensive and frustrating.

Here's our guide to planning business travel that pays for itself.

## Part 1: Trade fair and buying trips

### Choose the right destination

| Destination | Known for |
|---|---|
| **Turkey (Istanbul)** | Textiles and clothing, home goods, furniture, building materials, food products |
| **Dubai (UAE)** | Electronics, auto parts, general trading, re-export hub |
| **China (Guangzhou, Yiwu)** | Almost everything: electronics, machinery, small goods, the Canton Fair |
| **India** | Pharmaceuticals, machinery, textiles, auto parts |
| **Kenya (Nairobi)** | Regional distribution, manufactured goods |

### Plan around the fair dates

Major trade fairs run on fixed calendars, often once or twice a year. Book **at least 6–8 weeks ahead** to allow time for:

- **Visa processing.** Many business visas need an **invitation letter** from the fair organiser or a supplier.
- **Expo registration**, which is often free or discounted if completed in advance.
- **Flights and hotels**, which fill up and rise in price during fair weeks.

### Prepare to buy

1. **Know your numbers.** Know your target landed cost, meaning the purchase price plus shipping, duties and clearing, before you negotiate.
2. **Bring samples or photos** of what sells in your market.
3. **Collect business cards and catalogues**, then compare suppliers at your hotel in the evening.
4. **Never pay large deposits in cash to individuals.** Use traceable bank transfers to registered companies.
5. **Inspect before shipping**, or use an inspection service for large orders.

### Get your goods home

Shipping is where many traders lose money. Plan it before you buy:

- **Air cargo:** fast but expensive, best for small, high-value goods.
- **Sea freight:** cheaper for volume, shipped to Mombasa or Dar es Salaam, then trucked to Kampala.
- **Consolidation:** combine smaller orders from several suppliers into one shipment to save money.
- **Clearing:** factor in Uganda Revenue Authority duties, VAT and clearing agent fees.

A good travel partner connects you with **reliable cargo agents** before you travel.

## Part 2: Corporate and conference travel

### Set a simple travel policy

Even a small company benefits from a short written policy:

- Who can approve travel?
- What class of flights is allowed (economy, or business for long haul)?
- Hotel budget per night, by city
- Per diems and expense rules
- How far in advance trips must be booked

### Centralise bookings

When staff book their own travel, costs and records get scattered. Booking everything through one agency gives you:

- **Better fares** through comparison and negotiated rates
- **One invoice** and clear records for audits and donors
- **Duty of care**, because you always know where your travellers are
- **Fast help** when flights are cancelled or plans change

### Hosting international visitors

If partners, investors or board members visit Uganda, make the trip memorable. Add a **gorilla trek in Bwindi** or a short **safari in Murchison Falls** after the meetings. It's an impressive way to show your country and strengthen the relationship.

## Part 3: Team retreats and incentives

Annual planning retreats and incentive trips are a powerful way to motivate staff. Popular options from Kampala include:

- **Jinja:** the Nile, adventure activities, and two hours from Kampala
- **Lake Bunyonyi and Kabale:** a calm lake and cool weather
- **Entebbe:** lakeside resorts, close to the city
- **Mombasa, Zanzibar or Dubai:** for incentive trips that reward top performers
