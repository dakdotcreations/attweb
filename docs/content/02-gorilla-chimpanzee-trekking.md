## An hour you will remember for the rest of your life

Nothing quite prepares you for it. After hours of hiking through misty rainforest, the tracker raises a hand, the undergrowth parts, and a silverback mountain gorilla looks back at you, calm and enormous, a few metres away.

Uganda is home to **around half of the world's remaining mountain gorillas**, and Bwindi Impenetrable National Park is the best place on Earth to see them. Uganda is also one of Africa's leading chimpanzee destinations, so you can see both of our closest relatives in one trip.

## Gorilla trekking in Bwindi and Mgahinga

**Bwindi Impenetrable National Park** has several habituated gorilla families spread across four sectors: Buhoma, Ruhija, Rushaga and Nkuringo. Treks start each morning after a ranger briefing. Depending on where the family is, the walk can take from one to six hours. Once you reach them, you spend **one full hour** with the group.

**Mgahinga Gorilla National Park**, on the slopes of the Virunga volcanoes, offers gorilla trekking and the rare **golden monkey** in a dramatic volcanic setting.

## Chimpanzee tracking in Kibale and beyond

**Kibale Forest National Park** has the highest density of primates in East Africa and is the best place in Uganda to track chimpanzees. Habituated groups are noisy, playful and quick, so expect to hear them before you see them. Kibale also has 12 other primate species, including red colobus and L'Hoest's monkeys.

We also arrange chimpanzee tracking in **Kyambura Gorge** (Queen Elizabeth NP) and **Budongo Forest** (near Murchison Falls).

### Content

Meeting a mountain gorilla family in the wild is one of the most moving wildlife experiences on the planet. It's also one that rewards good preparation. These are the questions we get asked most often, answered by our team in Kampala.

## Where can you trek gorillas?

Mountain gorillas live in only three countries: **Uganda, Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of Congo**. Uganda has two gorilla parks:

- **Bwindi Impenetrable National Park:** home to roughly half of the world's mountain gorillas, with many habituated families across four sectors (Buhoma, Ruhija, Rushaga and Nkuringo).
- **Mgahinga Gorilla National Park:** a smaller park on the Virunga volcanoes, with one habituated family and golden monkeys.

Uganda's gorilla permits cost less than Rwanda's, which is one reason many travellers choose Bwindi.

## How do gorilla permits work?

- Every trekker needs a **gorilla permit**, issued by the Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA).
- Each habituated family can be visited by **a maximum of eight people per day**, so permits are limited.
- **Prices are set by UWA** and differ for foreign non-residents, foreign residents and East African citizens. Rates change from time to time, so ask us for the current price when you enquire.
- The permit covers the trek, the rangers and **one hour with the gorillas**.
- You must be **at least 15 years old** to trek.

**Book early.** For June to September and December to February, we recommend securing permits **3–6 months ahead**. Your permit is tied to a specific date and sector, so we'll confirm it before arranging the rest of your trip.

## How fit do you need to be?

You don't need to be an athlete, but you should be comfortable walking for several hours on steep, muddy, uneven ground at altitude (Bwindi sits at roughly 1,160–2,600 metres).

- Treks last **anywhere from 1 to 6 hours**, depending on where the gorillas are that day.
- Rangers try to match groups to families by fitness. Tell them at the briefing if you'd prefer a shorter walk.
- **Hire a porter.** For a small fee, a local porter carries your bag and helps you up and down slopes. It makes a real difference, and it supports the communities around the park.
- Walking sticks are provided at the start.

## The rules on the trail

These rules protect the gorillas, which are vulnerable to human illnesses:

1. Do not trek if you have a cold, flu or any infectious illness.
2. Keep a distance of **at least 7 metres** from the gorillas.
3. Wear a face mask when asked by rangers.
4. Speak quietly, and don't eat or drink near the gorillas.
5. No flash photography.
6. If a gorilla approaches you, stay calm, stay still and follow the ranger.

## What to pack for gorilla trekking

**Clothing**
- Waterproof, broken-in hiking boots with good grip
- Long trousers and a long-sleeved shirt (to protect against nettles and insects)
- A light rain jacket, because it can rain at any time of year
- Gardening gloves, for grabbing vines and branches
- Gaiters, or socks tucked over your trousers
- A warm fleece for cool mornings

**In your daypack**
- At least 2 litres of water
- A packed lunch (provided by your lodge) and energy snacks
- Insect repellent and sunscreen
- A camera with a spare battery. Rainforest light is low, so a fast lens helps.
- A waterproof cover or dry bag for your camera and phone

**Documents**
- Your passport (checked against your permit at the briefing)
- Your yellow fever vaccination certificate
